Life Cycle:-
-> 1. Pre-Case / Initiation
Issue arises (dispute, crime, breach of contract, etc.)
Investigation (by police, regulators, or lawyers)
Legal advice sought
Demand notice / complaint may be sent (civil cases)

-> 2. Filing the Case
Plaintiff/Prosecution files:
Civil: Complaint / Plaint
Criminal: Charge sheet / Information / Indictment
Case is registered in court
Summons or warrant issued to the defendant/accused

-> 3. Pleadings / Responses
Defendant responds:
Written statement / Answer
Motion to dismiss (if applicable)
Court identifies issues in dispute

-> 4. Pre-Trial Stage
Discovery / Evidence exchange
Interrogations, depositions, affidavits
Interim applications (bail, injunctions, stay orders)
Settlement / mediation attempts (mainly civil cases)

-> 5. Trial
Opening arguments
Examination & cross-examination of witnesses
Presentation of evidence
Final arguments

-> 6. Judgment / Verdict
Judge or jury delivers decision
Determines:
Liability or guilt
Compensation or punishment

-> 7. Post-Judgment Stage
Sentencing (criminal cases)
Execution of decree (civil cases)
Compliance or enforcement

-> 8. Appeal / Review (Optional)
Aggrieved party may file:
Appeal
Revision
Review

Higher court may:
Uphold
Modify
Reverse the decision

-> 9. Closure
Case fully resolved
Records archived
Rights and obligations settled

1. Judgment by the Lower Court

The lower court delivers its final order/judgment.
The losing party decides whether there are legal grounds to appeal (error of law, procedure, jurisdiction, etc.).

2. Decision to Appeal & Legal Grounds

The appellant (person appealing) identifies:
Errors of law (most common)
Procedural irregularities
Misinterpretation of evidence (limited in many systems)
Appeals are not a full retrial in most cases.

3. Filing Notice of Appeal

A Notice of Appeal / Petition of Appeal is filed in:
The lower court or
The appellate court (depends on jurisdiction)
Must be filed within a strict time limit (e.g., 30–90 days).
Court fees are paid.

4. Preparation of the Record

The lower court sends the case record to the higher court:
Pleadings
Evidence
Transcripts
Judgment/order
No new evidence is usually allowed.

5. Submission of Written Arguments

Appellant’s brief: explains why the lower court was wrong.
Respondent’s reply: defends the lower court’s decision.
Sometimes a rejoinder is allowed.

6. Admission / Preliminary Hearing (if applicable)

The higher court may:
Admit the appeal
Dismiss it at the threshold
Limit the issues to be heard
Common in constitutional or supreme courts.

7. Oral Arguments

Lawyers present arguments before judges.
Judges may ask questions.
Focus is mainly on law, not facts.

8. Judgment by the Higher Court

The appellate court may:
✅ Uphold the lower court’s decision
🔁 Modify the decision
❌ Set aside the judgment
🔄 Remand the case back to the lower court

9. Further Appeal (Optional)

If permitted, a party may:
Appeal to an even higher court
File a review or revision
File a special leave / discretionary appeal
